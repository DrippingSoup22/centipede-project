# Terrain (from run 25)

Agreed with the user on 2026-10-24.

The body learned to walk and follow a line on a flat floor (runs 01 to 23).
Real centipedes walk on uneven ground, and change their wave and their
speed with it (Diaz et al. 2023; Kuroda et al. 2022). From run 25 the body
learns on real ground at its true size: every episode starts at a random
point of a ground drawn from a fixed mix, from flat to forest stones, where
the body may slow down but is never trapped (the user).

## A terrain file

`[environment.simulation] terrain_path` names a MuJoCo file whose world
shapes are the ground. The physics simulation joins it to the body's model
once, at load (`simulation.model_spec`, MuJoCo's model editing):

1. the terrain file is attached to the model's world, its names prefixed
   with `terrain_`;
2. every terrain shape takes the model floor's contact settings (friction,
   softness, the floor's category), so the feet meet it as they met the
   floor;
3. the floor is deleted.

Without `terrain_path` the body stands on the model's own floor, as before.
The joined model, with the terrain's data files, goes into every recording,
so a replay shows the same ground.

**Every leg link touches the ground.** On the floor the upper links never
came near it in a walk (run 23), and leaving them out saves about 1% of the
physics time. On the park's sand and pebbles the scripted walk's upper
links went through pebbles in 9 to 18% of its actions, up to 4 mm deep
(`archive/terrain_prototypes/link_check.py`): a leg sweeping through stones
would teach a walk no real body has.

**A foot's load is the weight it carries.** The carry, the stance measures
and the units' inputs read each foot's and the belly's load. On the floor
it was the contact's normal force; on stones a foot pressed against a
stone's side, or wedged between two, has a large normal force and carries
little: in the ledger such feet read up to 62 times half their unit's
weight over a second, and the carry they cost the front unit, which meets
every stone first, made a front unit with a leg held up, or dragged, pay
more than one that walks. The load is now the vertical part of the
ground's force on the shape, the weight it holds up (from 2026-10-24): on
the floor the same number, on a 20° slope at rest the body's whole weight,
where the normal forces sum to 0.94 of it.

## The park

`models/terrain_park.xml`, with its heights in `models/terrain_park.hfield`
(generated by `archive/terrain_prototypes/make_park.py` in the study's
Centipede folder): nine grounds of 3 m × 3 m in one height field of 3 mm
cells, 9 m × 9 m, inside a flat border 1.5 m wide.

```text
          west                                        east
         ┌────────────────┬────────────────┬────────────────┐  north
         │ dry_ground_01  │ gravel_ground  │ aerial_ground  │
         │ dry mud, easy  │ _01, easy      │ _rock, easy    │
         ├────────────────┼────────────────┼────────────────┤
         │ procedural     │ flat           │ dry_ground_    │
         │ ground A, easy │ easy           │ rocks, medium  │
         ├────────────────┼────────────────┼────────────────┤
         │ forest_ground_ │ coast_sand_04  │ rock_ground_02 │
         │ 04, hard       │ sand and       │ loose stones,  │
         │                │ pebbles, hard  │ medium         │
         └────────────────┴────────────────┴────────────────┘  south
              each 3 m; a start area of 1.5 m in each middle
```

- **Seven real grounds** are photogrammetry scans from Poly Haven, free of
  any rights (CC0), at their true size. A scan's height map stores only
  0 to 1; its scale was found by fitting the scan's normal map, which
  records the surface's true tilt, against the height map's slopes. Each
  ground is the middle 3 m of its scan, its overall tilt removed.
- **Ground A** is procedural (`archive/terrain_prototypes/make_terrains.py`):
  hills and hollows up to 8 mm, bumps up to 4 mm and holes up to 3 mm, no
  slope steeper than 36°.
- **Every ground fades** to the common level 0 over the 40 cm along its
  edges, so neighbours meet without a step; the border lies at level 0.

| Ground | Class | Relief | 90% of slopes under | Slopes over 30° |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| flat | easy | 0 | 0° | 0% |
| procedural (ground A) | easy | 16 mm | 9° | 0% |
| gravel_ground_01 | easy | 29 mm | 15° | 0.9% |
| dry_ground_01 (dry mud) | easy | 35 mm | 10° | 1.5% |
| aerial_ground_rock (hills) | easy | 111 mm | 12° | 0.3% |
| dry_ground_rocks | medium | 44 mm | 22° | 4.3% |
| rock_ground_02 (loose stones) | medium | 40 mm | 27° | 7.3% |
| coast_sand_04 (sand and pebbles) | hard | 79 mm | 30° | 10.3% |
| forest_ground_04 (forest stones) | hard | 95 mm | 29° | 9.1% |

**Why 3 mm cells.** The body is 66 mm long and its units 6.6 mm apart: 3 mm
cells keep the stones a foot steps on, at a cost that 5 mm cells hardly
lower ([the cost](#the-cost)).

## A start

Each start area is a square box site, `start_<ground>`, in the terrain file.
At every restart a world draws a ground with `[environment.start_area_weights]`
and a point uniformly in its area. The body faces a way drawn within
`start_heading_range_deg` (180 in run 25, any way) among those whose straight
walk of a whole episode at the command, 1.34 m, stays over the height field:
the first of 16 draws that does. The line is laid along the body
([task.md](task.md)), so it leads over the ground even from a start at the
park's edge. The body is set down there, 1 mm above the highest ground
within its reach.

**The border is a safety net.** A body that walks faster than the command,
or a start whose 16 draws all face the edge (once in 10,000 at the worst
place), still has flat ground under it for 1.5 m.

**The mix, fixed for the whole run** (the user, 2026-10-24): half easy
(flat, ground A, gravel, dry mud, the aerial hills), a third medium (dry
rocks, loose stones), a sixth hard (sand and pebbles, forest stones). No
ground levels in the run: the legs learn the wave from the units ahead,
and a ground changing under a forming wave would break what they had
learned.

## What the grounds ask of a walk

The ledger's scripted walk on each ground (the study's
`Training/analysis/ledger.py grounds`, straight lines, 16 worlds, 6 s;
`Training/archive/ledger/grounds_run25.txt`):

| Ground | The walk as on the floor | Seconds stalled, belly's load | Its body held higher | Seconds stalled, belly's load |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| floor | 81 mm/s | 0%, 0% | 89 mm/s | 0%, 0% |
| flat | 1.01 | 0%, 0% | 0.99 | 0%, 0% |
| procedural | 0.90 | 0%, 7% | 0.96 | 0%, 1% |
| gravel | 0.84 | 0%, 10% | 0.91 | 0%, 2% |
| dry mud | 0.79 | 7%, 9% | 0.85 | 6%, 2% |
| aerial hills | 0.81 | 5%, 6% | 0.84 | 4%, 2% |
| dry rocks | 0.35 | 29%, 39% | 0.69 | 7%, 10% |
| loose stones | 0.34 | 35%, 41% | 0.73 | 5%, 10% |
| sand and pebbles | 0.29 | 51%, 48% | 0.58 | 16%, 19% |
| forest stones | 0.21 | 51%, 38% | 0.45 | 15%, 20% |
| **the mix** | **0.59** | | **0.78** | |

Speeds on the grounds are shares of the same walk's on the floor; a second
is stalled when a world advances less than 10 mm in it.

- **The posture decides.** v6's belly is 2.2 mm above the ground: walking as
  on the floor, the body rests on stones for 40% of the steps on medium
  ground. With the lifts aimed 12° lower in stance, the body held higher,
  it crosses every ground: medium at 0.7 of its floor speed, hard at about
  half, stalled 15% of the seconds at most. The agents command those lifts
  directly. Energy for each mm rises from 0.037 mJ on the floor to 0.055
  on medium ground and 0.07 to 0.09 on hard.
- **A walk learned on the floor does not carry over.** Run 22's final walk
  keeps 0.37 of its floor speed over the mix and is stopped on medium and
  hard ground (67 to 94% of the seconds stalled): its short, low steps meet
  the stones. Run 25 learns on the ground from the start.
- **Every ground is kept at its true size.** The hard grounds are slow but
  crossable, as for real centipedes (Diaz et al. 2023), and are a sixth of
  the episodes.

## The gates on the mix

The curves' start and the energy price's rise wait for the noise-free walk
to reach a speed, 50 mm/s on the floor ([task.md](task.md),
[reward.md](reward.md)). On the mix every walk is slower, so the gates sit
where the same walk reaches them: the ledger's walk slowed to the gate's
speed on the floor, walked on every ground (the study's
`Training/archive/ledger/gate_check.txt`, straight lines, 16 worlds a
ground):

| Walk | The floor | The mix | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| The good walk at the gate's speed | 53 mm/s | 34.7 mm/s | 0.65 |
| The same, its body held higher | 54 mm/s | 43.4 mm/s | 0.80 |
| Legs in random phase (no wave) | 73 mm/s | 49.5 mm/s | 0.68 |
| Legs in step | 5.5 mm/s | 2.2 mm/s | |

In run 25 the curves start at **33 mm/s** (50 × 0.65) and the energy price
rises from a line share of **0.36** (0.55 × 0.65). The gates wait for the
speed only: the economy is what the rising price teaches (in run 22 it fell
from 0.30 to 0.07 mJ for each mm in training while the price rose and the
speed held), and run 23's economy threshold held its speed level 3 for its
last 170 cycles. As on the floor, a walk without a wave passes the speed
gate; the curves also wait for the wave to start forming.

## The ledger on the mix

With the loads read as the weight carried, the ledger's good walk (its
body held higher, on the command) pays more than every fault on the mix,
on straight lines and on the sharpest curves (the study's
`Training/archive/ledger/ledger_25_*.txt`), as it does on the floor.

## The cost

On a 5090, 4,352 worlds, one 20 ms action (2026-10-24,
`archive/terrain_speed/` in the study's Training folder):

| Ground | ms | Against the floor |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| The model's floor (a plane) | 183 | — |
| A flat height field | 279 | +53% |
| **3 mm, every leg link touching (chosen)** | **300** | **+65%** |
| 3 mm, only the foot's link | 296 | +62% |
| 3 mm, looser convex checks | 266 | +45% |
| 5 mm cells | 281 | +54% |
| 3 mm, only the feet touching | 242 | +32% |

A height field costs for every pair of shapes that could touch, rough or
flat (MuJoCo Warp checks each against the prisms under it), so most of the
cost is the height field itself.

- **Looser convex checks rejected**: on the forest ground they turned about
  0.05% of contact normals the wrong way
  (`archive/terrain_prototypes/ccd_accuracy.py`); MuJoCo's own settings are
  kept.
- **Only the feet touching rejected**: a trunk that passes through stones
  would teach a walk no real body has. MuJoCo Playground's rough-ground
  tasks let only the feet touch (Zakka et al. 2025), on robots whose trunk
  stays well above the ground.
- **Boxes in place of the trunk's shapes rejected**: under them more than
  50 prisms at once overflowed MuJoCo Warp's height-field limit and the
  physics went non-finite.

A Pascal GPU (the laptop's MX330) cannot build MuJoCo Warp's convex check
of a capsule against a height field (its kernel needs more than 4 KB of
parameters); the park runs on Volta or newer.

## Left out

- **The leaf litter** (`forest_leaves_02`): leaves are not rigid, and a
  rigid map of them makes walls up to its whole relief.
- **A ground that moves or gives**: sand and pebbles are rigid here.
